# Running Workout Library

## Easy Run

**ID:** `run-easy`

**Purpose:** Aerobic development, recovery between harder sessions and sustainable weekly volume.

**Intensity:** RPE 2–4. Conversational effort. Pace is normally a range or ceiling, not a target to chase.

**Typical volume:** 30–90 minutes depending on experience and training phase.

**Recovery demand:** Low.

**Compatible with:** Upper-body strength; mobility; easy cycling.

**Avoid or modify when:** Illness, worsening pain, or unusual fatigue makes even conversational running feel strained.

**Progression:** Add duration gradually before adding pace. Strides may be added when freshness is good.

**Common mistakes:** Running moderate instead of easy; forcing pace in heat or fatigue.

**Tags:** `running`, `aerobic`, `easy`, `low-fatigue`

---

## Recovery Run

**ID:** `run-recovery`

**Purpose:** Gentle movement after demanding training without adding meaningful intensity.

**Intensity:** RPE 1–3. Slower than normal easy pace when required.

**Typical volume:** 20–50 minutes.

**Recovery demand:** Very low.

**Compatible with:** Mobility and light upper-body training.

**Avoid or replace with rest when:** Gait changes, pain increases, or fatigue is severe.

**Progression:** Do not progress for its own sake; the session exists to support recovery.

**Tags:** `running`, `recovery`, `low-fatigue`

---

## Easy Run with Strides

**ID:** `run-easy-strides`

**Purpose:** Aerobic volume plus relaxed neuromuscular speed.

**Intensity:** Easy running with 4–8 × 15–25 second controlled strides; full easy recovery.

**Typical volume:** 35–75 minutes total.

**Recovery demand:** Low to moderate.

**Compatible with:** Upper-body strength; a following easy day.

**Avoid or modify when:** Calf, hamstring or Achilles symptoms are present.

**Progression:** Add stride repetitions before lengthening them; keep mechanics relaxed.

**Tags:** `running`, `easy`, `strides`, `neuromuscular`

---

## Continuous Tempo

**ID:** `run-tempo-continuous`

**Purpose:** Improve lactate-threshold endurance and sustained pace control.

**Intensity:** RPE 7–8; comfortably hard. Use current threshold capability, not unsupported goal pace.

**Typical quality volume:** 15–40 minutes continuous, surrounded by warm-up and cool-down.

**Recovery demand:** Moderate to high; usually 24–48 hours before another demanding run.

**Compatible with:** Upper-body strength later the same day when well tolerated.

**Avoid or modify when:** Recovery is poor, heat is extreme, or the required pace cannot be held without racing the session.

**Progression:** Increase controlled duration before increasing speed.

**Common mistakes:** Starting too fast; turning the final section into a time trial.

**Tags:** `running`, `quality`, `tempo`, `threshold`

---

## Cruise Intervals

**ID:** `run-threshold-cruise`

**Purpose:** Accumulate threshold work with short recoveries and better pace control than a continuous tempo.

**Intensity:** RPE 7–8. Examples include 3 × 10 minutes, 4 × 2 km or 5–6 × 1 mile with short jogging recovery.

**Typical quality volume:** 20–45 minutes.

**Recovery demand:** Moderate to high.

**Progression:** Add total threshold time or reduce recovery slightly before increasing pace.

**Tags:** `running`, `quality`, `threshold`, `intervals`

---

## VO2-Max Intervals

**ID:** `run-vo2-intervals`

**Purpose:** Develop high-end aerobic power and running economy.

**Intensity:** RPE 8–9. Repetitions commonly last 2–5 minutes with controlled jogging recovery.

**Typical quality volume:** 12–30 minutes.

**Recovery demand:** High; avoid placing near another hard run, heavy lower-body lifting or intense soccer.

**Progression:** Increase repeat count before making repeats faster. Stop when pace or mechanics deteriorate materially.

**Tags:** `running`, `quality`, `vo2max`, `intervals`

---

## Hill Repeats

**ID:** `run-hill-repeats`

**Purpose:** Running-specific strength, mechanics and aerobic power with less absolute speed.

**Intensity:** Depends on format: short powerful hills or longer controlled aerobic repetitions.

**Typical quality volume:** 8–25 minutes of uphill work.

**Recovery demand:** Moderate to high, especially for calves and posterior chain.

**Avoid or modify when:** Achilles, calf or hamstring symptoms are active.

**Tags:** `running`, `quality`, `hills`, `strength`

---

## Fartlek

**ID:** `run-fartlek`

**Purpose:** Flexible speed development with effort-based changes rather than rigid splits.

**Intensity:** Variable; hard portions normally RPE 6–9 depending on the workout purpose.

**Examples:** 10 × 1 minute on/off; 1-2-3-4-3-2-1 minute pyramid; mixed surges.

**Recovery demand:** Moderate to high.

**Tags:** `running`, `quality`, `fartlek`, `variety`

---

## Easy Long Run

**ID:** `run-long-easy`

**Purpose:** Aerobic endurance, musculoskeletal durability and fuelling practice.

**Intensity:** RPE 3–5; predominantly conversational.

**Typical volume:** Goal- and experience-dependent. Increase conservatively and account for total weekly load.

**Recovery demand:** Moderate to high based on duration.

**Compatible with:** Light upper-body strength; mobility.

**Avoid pairing with:** Heavy lower-body strength within the preceding 24–48 hours when long-run quality matters.

**Progression:** Increase duration gradually, then introduce controlled quality only after the distance is well tolerated.

**Tags:** `running`, `long-run`, `aerobic`, `endurance`

---

## Progressive Long Run

**ID:** `run-long-progressive`

**Purpose:** Endurance plus controlled late-run pace development.

**Intensity:** Begin easy; finish steady or moderately hard without racing.

**Recovery demand:** High.

**Progression:** Extend the steady finish gradually while preserving an easy opening section.

**Tags:** `running`, `long-run`, `progression`, `quality`

---

## Marathon-Pace Long Run

**ID:** `run-long-marathon-pace`

**Purpose:** Marathon-specific endurance, pacing, fuelling and confidence.

**Intensity:** Easy running plus one or more blocks at a realistic current marathon-effort range.

**Typical structure:** Continuous finish, separated blocks, or alternating steady and marathon effort.

**Recovery demand:** High to very high.

**Avoid or modify when:** Goal pace is unsupported by current fitness, recovery is poor, or environmental conditions make pace misleading.

**Progression:** Increase total marathon-pace volume gradually before using longer continuous blocks.

**Tags:** `running`, `long-run`, `marathon`, `race-specific`

---

## Tune-Up / Sharpening Session

**ID:** `run-sharpening`

**Purpose:** Maintain speed and rhythm while reducing overall training load near a race.

**Intensity:** Brief controlled quality; never a maximal test.

**Recovery demand:** Moderate but low total volume.

**Tags:** `running`, `taper`, `sharpening`, `race-specific`
