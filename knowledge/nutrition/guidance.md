# Nutrition Guidance Library

This library provides practical macro guidance and simple meal examples. It is not a calorie tracker and does not require detailed food logging. Targets are expressed as bodyweight-relative ranges so the dashboard can personalise them privately.

## Easy or Aerobic Run Day

**ID:** `nutrition-easy-run-day`

**Purpose:** Support normal aerobic training while maintaining recovery and appetite control.

**Protein:** 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day

**Carbohydrates:** 3–5 g/kg/day

**Fat:** 0.7–1.0 g/kg/day

**Hydration:** Normal daily hydration plus fluids lost during training.

**Simple examples:** Eggs and toast; yoghurt with fruit and oats; chicken and rice; salmon with potatoes; banana and protein yoghurt.

**Coach focus:** Keep protein consistent and place some carbohydrate before and after the run.

**Tags:** `nutrition`, `easy-run`, `aerobic`, `balanced`

## Recovery or Rest Day

**ID:** `nutrition-recovery-day`

**Purpose:** Maintain muscle repair and general recovery when training demand is low.

**Protein:** 1.8–2.2 g/kg/day

**Carbohydrates:** 2.5–4 g/kg/day

**Fat:** 0.8–1.0 g/kg/day

**Hydration:** Maintain normal hydration; replace any remaining losses from the previous day.

**Simple examples:** Omelette with toast; Greek yoghurt and fruit; lean meat with vegetables and rice; fish with potatoes; cottage cheese or protein yoghurt.

**Coach focus:** Keep protein high, reduce carbohydrate only modestly, and prioritise fruit and vegetables.

**Tags:** `nutrition`, `recovery`, `rest-day`, `protein`

## Interval or VO2 Session Day

**ID:** `nutrition-interval-day`

**Purpose:** Fuel a demanding quality session and replenish carbohydrate afterwards.

**Protein:** 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day

**Carbohydrates:** 4–6 g/kg/day

**Fat:** 0.6–0.9 g/kg/day

**Hydration:** Begin well hydrated and replace sweat losses after training.

**Simple examples:** Oats, banana and yoghurt; toast with eggs; rice bowl with chicken; burrito with extra rice and lean protein; pasta with lean beef; fruit and protein yoghurt.

**Coach focus:** Include an easy-to-digest carbohydrate meal or snack before training and a carbohydrate-plus-protein meal afterwards.

**Tags:** `nutrition`, `intervals`, `quality-session`, `high-carbohydrate`

## Tempo or Threshold Day

**ID:** `nutrition-tempo-day`

**Purpose:** Support sustained quality running without excessive gastrointestinal load.

**Protein:** 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day

**Carbohydrates:** 4–6 g/kg/day

**Fat:** 0.6–0.9 g/kg/day

**Hydration:** Normal daily hydration plus replacement of session losses.

**Simple examples:** Toast with eggs and fruit; oats and yoghurt; chicken and rice; tuna pasta; salmon with rice; banana before training and yoghurt afterwards.

**Coach focus:** Spread carbohydrate across the day and avoid a very high-fat meal immediately before the session.

**Tags:** `nutrition`, `tempo`, `threshold`, `quality-session`

## Long Run Day

**ID:** `nutrition-long-run-day`

**Purpose:** Provide sufficient carbohydrate before, during and after a long endurance session.

**Protein:** 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day

**Carbohydrates:** 5–7 g/kg/day

**Fat:** 0.6–0.9 g/kg/day

**Hydration:** Start hydrated, drink according to conditions and duration, and replace fluid and sodium afterwards.

**During training:** For longer runs, practise the same carbohydrate and hydration strategy intended for race day.

**Simple examples:** Toast, oats and banana before running; sports drink or gels during longer sessions; chicken and rice, pasta with lean meat, salmon and potatoes, fruit and yoghurt afterwards.

**Coach focus:** Prioritise carbohydrate availability and practise race-compatible foods rather than experimenting on key days.

**Tags:** `nutrition`, `long-run`, `endurance`, `high-carbohydrate`, `race-practice`

## Day Before a Long Run

**ID:** `nutrition-pre-long-run-day`

**Purpose:** Top up glycogen and hydration without requiring an extreme carb load.

**Protein:** 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day

**Carbohydrates:** 5–7 g/kg/day

**Fat:** 0.6–0.8 g/kg/day

**Hydration:** Drink regularly through the day and include normal dietary sodium.

**Simple examples:** Oats and fruit; rice with chicken; pasta with lean beef; bread or wraps; potatoes with fish; yoghurt, bananas and cereal as snacks.

**Coach focus:** Increase carbohydrate portions, keep meals familiar, and avoid turning dinner into an unusually large or greasy meal.

**Tags:** `nutrition`, `pre-long-run`, `carbohydrate`, `preparation`

**Protein:** 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day

## Double-Session Day

**ID:** `nutrition-double-session-day`

**Purpose:** Support two training sessions while maintaining recovery between them.

**Protein:** 1.8–2.2 g/kg/day

**Carbohydrates:** 5–7 g/kg/day

**Fat:** 0.6–0.9 g/kg/day

**Hydration:** Replace fluid and sodium after the first session before beginning the second.

**Simple examples:** Oats, toast and fruit; rice with chicken after session one; yoghurt and banana between sessions; pasta, potatoes or rice with lean protein at dinner.

**Coach focus:** Eat between sessions rather than trying to make up the entire day at dinner.

**Tags:** `nutrition`, `double-session`, `high-load`, `recovery`

## Race Week

**ID:** `nutrition-race-week`

**Purpose:** Maintain normal nutrition early in the week and gradually increase carbohydrate as race day approaches.

**Protein:** 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day

**Carbohydrates:** 4–7 g/kg/day, increasing toward the upper end near the event.

**Fat:** Moderate early in the week and slightly lower as carbohydrate portions rise.

**Hydration:** Maintain steady hydration rather than trying to correct everything the night before.

**Simple examples:** Familiar rice, pasta, bread, oats, fruit, potatoes, lean meat, fish and yoghurt-based meals.

**Coach focus:** Keep foods familiar, reduce unnecessary experimentation, and avoid overeating simply because training volume has dropped.

**Tags:** `nutrition`, `race-week`, `carbohydrate`, `taper`

---

## Marathon Carb-Loading Day

**ID:** `nutrition-carb-loading-day`

**Purpose:** Maximise carbohydrate availability before a marathon or similarly long race.

**Protein:** 1.4–1.8 g/kg/day

**Carbohydrates:** 7–10 g/kg/day when a deliberate carb load is appropriate.

**Fat:** Lower than normal to make room for carbohydrate without excessive total food volume.

**Hydration:** Drink normally and include sodium with meals; avoid forcing excessive plain water.

**Simple examples:** White rice, pasta, bread, cereal, oats, bananas, juice, low-fat yoghurt, lean chicken, simple sandwiches and familiar carbohydrate snacks.

**Coach focus:** Use lower-fibre, familiar carbohydrate foods and spread intake across the day.

**Tags:** `nutrition`, `carb-load`, `marathon`, `race-preparation`

## Race Morning

**ID:** `nutrition-race-morning`

**Purpose:** Provide familiar, digestible carbohydrate before racing.

**Protein:** Small to moderate amount if normally tolerated.

**Carbohydrates:** A carbohydrate-focused meal several hours before the start, scaled to body size and tolerance.

**Fat:** Low.

**Hydration:** Arrive hydrated without excessive last-minute drinking.

**Simple examples:** Toast with jam or honey; oats with banana; cereal with low-fat milk; sports drink; a familiar small coffee when normally used.

**Coach focus:** Nothing new on race morning. Use the exact foods and timing already tested in long runs.

**Tags:** `nutrition`, `race-morning`, `pre-race`, `carbohydrate`

---

## Post-Race or Very Long Session

**ID:** `nutrition-post-race-recovery`

**Purpose:** Begin carbohydrate replenishment, muscle repair and rehydration after a major endurance effort.

**Protein:** 1.8–2.2 g/kg/day

**Carbohydrates:** 5–8 g/kg/day depending on the event and how soon normal training resumes.

**Fat:** Normal moderate intake once appetite and digestion settle.

**Hydration:** Replace fluid and sodium progressively rather than all at once.

**Simple examples:** Recovery drink and banana; chicken and rice; burrito; pasta with lean meat; salmon and potatoes; yoghurt, fruit and cereal.

**Coach focus:** Start with easy-to-eat food, then return to normal balanced meals rather than relying only on snacks.

**Tags:** `nutrition`, `post-race`, `recovery`, `carbohydrate`, `protein`

## Hot-Weather Training Day

**ID:** `nutrition-hot-weather-day`

**Purpose:** Support training when fluid and electrolyte losses are likely to be elevated.

**Protein:** Use the range appropriate to the training session.

**Carbohydrates:** Use the range appropriate to the training session.

**Fat:** Use the range appropriate to the training session.

**Hydration:** Begin hydrated, drink to a practical plan during longer sessions, and replace fluid and sodium afterwards.

**Simple examples:** Water with meals; electrolyte drink around training; fruit, yoghurt, rice bowls, sandwiches and salty recovery meals.

**Coach focus:** Adjust fluid and sodium more than macros unless heat materially changes session duration or intensity.

**Tags:** `nutrition`, `heat`, `hydration`, `electrolytes`
