# Strength Workout Library

## Upper Push

**ID:** `strength-upper-push`

**Purpose:** Maintain or develop chest, shoulder and triceps strength with minimal direct lower-body fatigue.

**Typical exercises:** Bench press, incline dumbbell press, overhead press, lateral raise, triceps extension or band pushdown.

**Typical volume:** 12–18 working sets.

**Intensity:** Mostly 1–3 reps in reserve; avoid unnecessary failure when a key run follows within 24 hours.

**Recovery demand:** Moderate upper-body fatigue; low running interference.

**Compatible with:** Easy runs, recovery runs and many quality-run days when scheduled after running.

**Tags:** `strength`, `upper-body`, `push`, `low-running-interference`

## Upper Pull

**ID:** `strength-upper-pull`

**Purpose:** Back, rear-deltoid and biceps strength and muscle balance.

**Typical exercises:** Pull-up or pulldown, horizontal row, rear-delt work, curl variations.

**Typical volume:** 12–18 working sets.

**Recovery demand:** Moderate upper-body fatigue; generally low running interference.

**Compatible with:** Easy running and quality-run days when grip or back fatigue does not affect running posture.

**Tags:** `strength`, `upper-body`, `pull`, `low-running-interference`

---

## Arms

**ID:** `strength-arms`

**Purpose:** Direct biceps and triceps development with negligible leg fatigue.

**Typical exercises:** Barbell or EZ-bar curl, incline dumbbell curl, hammer curl, close-grip bench press, band pushdown and overhead band extension.

**Typical volume:** 12–18 working sets total.

**Intensity:** 1–2 reps in reserve on most sets; final isolation sets may approach failure when recovery is good.

**Recovery demand:** Low systemically; moderate locally.

**Compatible with:** Easy runs and the day before a long run, provided the session does not become excessively long or exhausting.

**Tags:** `strength`, `upper-body`, `arms`, `low-running-interference`

## Lower Strength

**ID:** `strength-lower`

**Purpose:** Maintain or build force production and tissue capacity for hips, knees, hamstrings and calves.

**Typical exercises:** Squat, Romanian deadlift, split squat, step-up, hamstring curl, calf raise and glute bridge.

**Typical volume:** 8–16 working sets depending on the training phase.

**Intensity:** Usually 1–3 reps in reserve. Marathon-focused phases should favour quality and consistency over maximal fatigue.

**Recovery demand:** High; commonly 24–72 hours depending on load, exercise selection and training age.

**Compatible with:** Easy or recovery running when well tolerated.

**Avoid pairing with:** Important intervals, marathon-pace long runs or races within the next 24–48 hours.

**Progression:** Add load or repetitions conservatively while monitoring soreness, gait and key-run quality.

**Tags:** `strength`, `lower-body`, `high-fatigue`, `running-interference`

## Marathon Maintenance Strength

**ID:** `strength-marathon-maintenance`

**Purpose:** Preserve strength and tissue capacity during high running volume with reduced soreness and interference.

**Typical exercises:** One squat or unilateral pattern, one hip-hinge or hamstring pattern, calf work and concise trunk work.

**Typical volume:** 5–10 working sets.

**Intensity:** Moderate to heavy but low volume; avoid grinding repetitions.

**Recovery demand:** Moderate.

**Progression:** Maintain load where possible; reduce sets before reducing useful intensity during peak running weeks.

**Tags:** `strength`, `lower-body`, `marathon`, `maintenance`

## Core and Trunk

**ID:** `strength-core`

**Purpose:** Trunk stiffness, load transfer and tolerance without large systemic fatigue.

**Typical exercises:** Side plank, dead bug, Pallof press, carry and controlled back-extension variants where appropriate.

**Typical volume:** 10–25 minutes.

**Recovery demand:** Low.

**Compatible with:** Most training days.

**Tags:** `strength`, `core`, `low-fatigue`

## Exercise substitution principles

- Match the movement pattern and target muscles before matching the exact exercise.
- Prefer pain-free range and stable technique over preserving the planned load.
- Bands can replace cable pushdowns and overhead extensions.
- Dumbbells can replace barbell pressing and curling when equipment is limited.
- A substitution should not quietly increase lower-body or spinal fatigue before a key run.
