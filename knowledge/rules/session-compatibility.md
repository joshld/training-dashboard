# Session Compatibility Rules

These rules guide plan generation and simulations. They are defaults, not absolute medical or performance claims.

## Running and strength

- Prefer upper-body strength after a quality run rather than before it when both occur on the same day.
- Avoid heavy lower-body strength within 24–48 hours before a key interval, tempo, race-practice or long-run session.
- During high-volume marathon phases, preserve useful strength intensity while reducing lower-body set count and failure training.
- Count severe lower-body soreness, altered gait or pain as reasons to regress or defer load.

## Quality-session spacing

- Normally separate demanding running sessions by at least one lower-load day.
- Treat futsal and competitive soccer as quality/high-impact sessions for spacing purposes.
- Avoid adding a third demanding session simply because its sport label differs.

## Long runs

- Protect the long run when it is the week's primary marathon-specific stimulus.
- Reduce an embedded pace block before reducing the whole long-run distance when fatigue is elevated but aerobic volume remains tolerable.
- Use effort, heart rate and environmental context when heat, hills or wind make nominal pace misleading.

## Progression

- Progress one major variable at a time: duration, distance, repetition count, pace or recovery.
- Prefer increasing controlled work before increasing speed.
- Hold or regress a workout when execution quality, mechanics or recovery meaningfully deteriorate.

## User control

- Suggestions must explain the trade-off.
- Simulations never alter the active plan until explicitly applied.
- When data is incomplete, show assumptions and use conservative recommendations.
